## Full notes: what ELPS means

ELPS is a way to move from a concrete experience to a written mathematical symbol. The curriculum describes four connected parts: **Experience, Language, Pictures and Symbols**.

**Experience:** Begin with something a child can handle and count. The syllabus uses counting concrete objects as its example. The point is to give the number a meaning before introducing its written form.

**Language:** Ask the child to describe what was counted and how many there were. The spoken explanation connects the experience with number words.

**Pictures:** Represent the objects in a picture. In the curriculum’s example, two flowers can be represented by two pictures of flowers.

**Symbols:** Connect that experience and picture to the written numeral **2**. The numeral is a compact representation of the quantity already explored.

## A classroom example

This is an adapted example, not an additional official task. Put two flowers in front of the child. Let the child count them, describe the quantity, draw two flowers and then connect the drawing to the numeral 2. Ask the child to explain the connection instead of only copying the numeral.

## Short notes

| Part | What it involves |
| --- | --- |
| E — Experience | Count concrete objects. |
| L — Language | Say what was counted and how many. |
| P — Pictures | Represent the quantity pictorially. |
| S — Symbols | Use the corresponding written numeral. |

## Quick revision

**Handle → say → draw → write.** This is a memory aid for the sequence, not the official expansion of ELPS.

**Key terms:** concrete objects, spoken language, pictorial representation, written symbol.

## Questions worth preparing

- **Very short:** Expand ELPS.
- **Short answer:** What does the language stage add to a counting activity?
- **Descriptive:** Explain ELPS using the curriculum’s two-flower example.
- **Compare:** How does pictorial representation differ from the written symbol in this example?
- **Application:** A child copies “2” without connecting it to two objects. How could you revisit the experience, language and picture stages?

These are practice prompts, not predicted examination questions.

## MCQ for review

A child has counted two flowers and described the quantity. Which action corresponds to the **P** in ELPS?

A. Drawing two flowers  
B. Copying the numeral 2 repeatedly  
C. Repeating the word “two”  
D. Counting a different group of real flowers

**Answer: A.** Pictures represent the experienced quantity pictorially; the numeral belongs to the symbol stage.
