Unit 1: Introduction to Yoga and History of Yoga

Etymological Origins, Aims, Scope, Demystifying Misconceptions & School Pedagogy

Weightage: $5\text{ Marks}$

1.1 & 1.2 Etymology, Classical Definitions & Historical Trajectory

Vedic to Contemporary Era

The word Yoga originates from the Sanskrit root verb 'Yuj' (युज्), which signifies to join, to yoke, to unite, or to harness. Epistemologically, Yoga represents the experiential union of the individual finite consciousness (Jivatma) with the supreme universal consciousness (Paramatma). Rather than a modern fitness fad, Yoga is an ancient, science-backed psycho-philosophical technology engineered for the harmonization of body, breath, mind, intellect, and spirit:

Maharshi Patanjali:
"योगश्चিত্তवृत्ति निरोधঃ"
(Yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ)

"Yoga is the intentional cessation, quieting, and mastery of the fluctuations, modifications, and agitations of the mind-stuff (Chitta)."

Bhagavad Gita (Lord Krishna):
"যোগঃ কর্মসু কৌশলম্"
(Yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam)

"Yoga is supreme dexterity, wisdom, mindfulness, and selfless excellence in all actions and duties." (Chapter $2$, Verse $50$).

Equanimity of Mind:
"সমত্বং যোগ উচ্যতে"
(Samatvaṁ yoga ucyate)

"Evenness, emotional equilibrium, and balance in triumph or defeat, pleasure or sorrow, is designated as Yoga."

Historical Epoch Foundational Treatises & Key Figures Doctrinal Focus & Evolution
1. Pre-Vedic & Vedic Era (c. $3000\text{--}1500\text{ BCE}$) Indus-Saraswati Seals (Pashupati in yogic posture); Rigveda, Atharvaveda. Hymns of spiritual aspiration, solar adoration, tapas (austerity), and meditative absorption through sacred rituals.
2. Pre-Classical Period (c. $1500\text{--}500\text{ BCE}$) Upanishads (Katha, Maitri, Shvetashvatara); Mahabharata and Bhagavad Gita. Yoga internalized as contemplation of the Self (Atman); synthesis of Karma Yoga, Jnana Yoga, and Bhakti Yoga.
3. Classical Period (c. $500\text{ BCE}\text{--}200\text{ CE}$) Maharshi Patanjali's Yoga Sutras ($196\text{ aphorisms}$ in $4\text{ chapters}$). Standardization of Raja Yoga and the Eight-Limbed (Ashtanga) system; philosophical dualism of Purusha (pure consciousness) and Prakriti (matter).
4. Post-Classical & Hatha Yoga (c. $500\text{--}1800\text{ CE}$) Matsyendranath, Gorakhnath; Hatha Yoga Pradipika (Swami Swatmarama), Gheranda Samhita. Shift towards optimizing the physical vessel (Deha) through asanas, shatkriyas, pranayama, mudras, and bandhas to awaken Kundalini energy.
5. Modern Era ($1893\text{--Present}$) Swami Vivekananda ($1893\text{ Chicago Parliament}$), T. Krishnamacharya, B.K.S. Iyengar, Swami Sivananda. Global democratization of yoga; scientific biomedical validation; International Yoga Day celebrated globally every year on June 21!

1.4 Dismantling Prevalent Misconceptions About Yoga

  • • Myth 1: "Yoga is a Religious Dogma": Reality: Yoga is a universal, secular science of mind-body optimization. It requires no conversion, worships no exclusive sectarian deity, and operates via empirical physiology.
  • • Myth 2: "Yoga is Mere Physical Contortionism/Gymnastics": Reality: Asanas are merely the $3\text{rd}$ step of an $8\text{-fold}$ psycho-spiritual ladder. A person can perform a perfect headstand and still be completely un-yogic if their mind is consumed by greed and rage!
  • • Myth 3: "Yoga is Only for Sanyasis, Ascetics & The Elderly": Reality: Yoga is designed for householders, school children, and active professionals to maintain equilibrium amidst worldly responsibilities.
  • • Myth 4: "Yoga Requires Extreme Ascetic Starvation": Reality: The Bhagavad Gita emphasizes moderation: "Yoga is not for one who overeats, nor for one who fasts excessively."
  • • Myth 5: "Yoga is Too Slow to Provide Health Benefits": Reality: Yoga works profoundly on the endocrine, autonomic, and nervous systems, offering deep restorative, metabolic, and cardiovascular longevity.
Yoga is not about touching your toes; it is about what you learn on the way down!

1.3 & 1.5 Scope & Importance of Yoga in School Education

Incorporating daily yogic practices in elementary schooling addresses classroom crises of attention deficit, anxiety, postural degeneration, and behavioral turbulence:

  • • "Prevention is Better Than Cure": Inculcating early lifestyle hygiene immunizes children against adolescent obesity, juvenile hypertension, and anxiety disorders.
  • • Cognitive & Scholastic Amplification: Mindful breath regulation increases oxygenation to the prefrontal cortex, enhancing memory encoding and auditory concentration.
  • • Emotional Regulation: Shifting the autonomic nervous system from sympathetic (fight-or-flight) to parasympathetic (rest-and-digest), mitigating schoolyard aggression.
  • • Postural Ergonomics: Neutralizing the postural deformities (kyphosis, slouching) caused by heavy school satchels and prolonged sedentary bench seating.
Curricular Age Progression (SCERT Guidelines):
• Under Age $5$: Light stretching, balancing play, animal imitation postures (Cat, Butterfly).
• Age $5\text{ to } 7$ (Ka-Shreni / Classes $1\text{--}2$): Structured Asanas and simple silent meditation circles.
• Age $8+$ (Classes $3$ onwards): Formal Pranayama (Anulom-Viloma, Bhastrika), Bandhas, and Mudras.

Unit 2: Classical Forms & Philosophical Streams of Yoga

Patanjali's Raja Yoga (Ashtanga Architecture), Hatha Yoga Systems & The Four Classical Paths

Weightage: $5\text{ Marks}$

2.1 Raja Yoga: Maharshi Patanjali's Eight-Limbed Architecture (অষ্টাঙ্গ যোগ)

$8\text{ Limbs of Raja Yoga}$

Raja Yoga (the "Royal Path") focuses directly on controlling the psyche and transcending the ego through ethical cultivation, meditative discipline, and mental absorption. Patanjali systematically codified this progressive transformation into eight sequential limbs (Ashtanga):

1. Yama (যম) Social Restraints

Five universal ethical harmonies with society:

  • • Ahimsa: Non-violence in deed, word, and thought.
  • • Satya: Truthfulness and integrity.
  • • Asteya: Non-stealing (material and intellectual).
  • • Brahmacharya: Moderation and chaste energy.
  • • Aparigraha: Non-possessiveness and non-greed.
2. Niyama (নিয়ম) Personal Observances

Five personal moral disciplines for inner purity:

  • • Saucha: Internal and external cleanliness.
  • • Santosha: Deep contentment with present state.
  • • Tapas: Fortitude, austerity, and persistent effort.
  • • Svadhyaya: Self-study and sacred reading.
  • • Ishvara Pranidhana: Surrender to supreme order.
3. Asana (আসন) Physical Poise

Defined as "Sthira Sukham Asanam" (steady and comfortable posture).

Postural stillness frees the mind from somatic disturbances and physical restlessness.

4. Pranayama (প্ৰাণায়াম) Breath Expansion

Regulation of the respiratory cycle (Inhalation, Retention, Exhalation).

Controls the vital life-force (Prana), acting as the direct physiological bridge to the brain.

5. Pratyahara (প্ৰত্যাহাৰ) Sensory Withdrawal

Withdrawing the $5\text{ cognitive senses}$ from external stimuli, like a tortoise retracting its limbs.

Prevents sensory distraction from disturbing internal contemplation.

6. Dharana (ধাৰণা) Focused Attention

Fixing the attention exclusively on a single spatial point or symbol (e.g., breath at the nose-tip, candle flame, or Ajna chakra).

7. Dhyana (ধ্যান) Meditation

An uninterrupted, seamless flow of awareness towards the object of concentration, like oil poured from one vessel to another.

8. Samadhi (সমাধি) Superconscious State

Total transcendental absorption where the meditator, the act of meditating, and the object of meditation dissolve into pure unity.

Bahiranga vs. Antaranga Yoga: Limbs $1\text{ to } 5$ (Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara) constitute the External Path (Bahiranga) preparatory disciplines; Limbs $6\text{ to } 8$ (Dharana, Dhyana, Samadhi) constitute the internal triad known as Samyama (Antaranga), representing direct psychological transcendence.

2.2 Hatha Yoga: Physiology of Energetic Balance & Link to Raja Yoga

Harmonizing Sun & Moon

The term Hatha is composed of two primordial monosyllables: 'Ha' (হ) signifying the Sun (Surya energy, representing the vital breath, prana, masculine principle, and right nostril / Pingala Nadi), and 'Tha' (ঠ) signifying the Moon (Chandra energy, representing the mind, consciousness, feminine principle, and left nostril / Ida Nadi). Hatha Yoga is the energetic science of balancing these opposing polarities to awaken the central spiritual conduit: the Sushumna Nadi.

Core Pillars of Hatha Yoga (Hatha Yoga Pradipika)
  • • Shatkriyas (Purificatory Cleansings): Detoxifying bodily fluids (Tridoshas: Vata, Pitta, Kapha) before attempting advanced postures.
  • • Asanas: Cultivating physical steadiness, suppleness, and metabolic vigor.
  • • Pranayama (Kumbhaka): Restraining breath to quiet mental restlessness.
  • • Mudras & Bandhas: Neuromuscular locks that seal energetic leakage and stimulate dormant Kundalini.
  • • Nadanusandhana: Internal meditative absorption on sacred inner acoustic vibrations.
The Symbiotic Link: Hatha Yoga as the Ladder to Raja Yoga

Swami Swatmarama clearly affirms in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika:

"Kevalam raja-yogaya hatha-vidyopadisyate"
(Hatha Yoga is taught solely as a stepping-stone ladder to reach Raja Yoga).

Attempting to control the agitated human mind directly through pure meditation (Raja Yoga) without preparing the physical body and breath (Hatha Yoga) is like trying to ride an untrained wild stallion. Physical purification and breath stabilization lay the indispensable bedrock for mental stillness!

Hatha Yoga is the Physical Foundation ➔ Raja Yoga is the Spiritual Pinnacle!

2.3 The Four Classical Spiritual Paths & Tantra Yoga

1. Karma Yoga (কৰ্মযোগ)

Path of Selfless Action

Anchored in the Bhagavad Gita's philosophy of Nishkama Karma—performing duty with total diligence while relinquishing selfish attachment to the fruits of action (Karmaphala). Teaches educators to teach with love without egoic demands for adulation!

2. Jnana Yoga (জ্ঞানযোগ)

Path of Philosophical Inquiry

The intellectual path of discerning the Real from the Unreal (Viveka), dispassionate detachment (Vairagya), and rigorous self-inquiry ("Who am I?" / Neti, Neti). Dissolves ignorance (Avidya) through enlightened wisdom.

3. Bhakti Yoga (ভক্তিযোগ)

Path of Pure Devotion

Channeling emotional energy into sublime, unconditional devotion, surrender (Prapatti), prayer, and chanting. Exemplified in Assam by Mahapurush Srimanta Sankardeva and Sri Sri Madhabdeva through the Neo-Vaishnavite Nam-Kirtan renaissance!

4. Tantra Yoga (তন্ত্ৰযোগ)

Path of Energetic Transformation

Utilizing sacred geometric diagrams (Yantras), vibrational syllables (Mantras), and psychosomatic energy chakras (Muladhara to Sahasrara) to expand consciousness and transform primal physical forces into transcendental spiritual vitality.

Unit 3: Yogic Practices Catalog: Asana, Pranayama, Mudra, Bandha & Kriya

Surya Namaskar, Postural Anatomies, Pranic Regulation, Neuro-Vascular Locks & Shatkarma Detoxification

3.1 & 3.2 The Master Asana Catalog: Postural Classification & Surya Namaskar

$12\text{ Steps of Surya Namaskar}$
☀️ Surya Namaskar (The $12\text{-Step}$ Solar Salutation) Complete Cardiovascular & Neuromuscular Flow

A dynamic sequential flow linking $12\text{ postures}$ harmonized with specific inhalation, exhalation, and solar mantras:

1. Pranamasana (Exhale)
2. Hastauttanasana (Inhale)
3. Padahastasana (Exhale)
4. Ashwa Sanchalan (Inhale)
5. Dandasana (Retain)
6. Ashtanga Namaskar (Exhale)
7. Bhujangasana (Inhale)
8. Parvatasana (Exhale)
9. Ashwa Sanchalan (Inhale)
10. Padahastasana (Exhale)
11. Hastauttana (Inhale)
12. Pranamasana (Exhale)
1. Standing Asanas (দণ্ডায়মান)
  • • Tadasana: Mountain pose; builds vestibular balance, elongates spine, corrects flat feet.
  • • Vrikshasana: Tree pose; cultivates proprioception and mental concentration.
  • • Pada Hastasana: Standing forward bend; stretches hamstrings, stimulates digestion.
  • • Ardhachakrasana: Half-wheel backward arch; expands lung volume and heart cavity.
2. Sitting Asanas (উপবিষ্ট)
  • • Padmasana & Sukhasana: Classical meditative seats; grounds pelvic floor, straightens spine.
  • • Vajrasana: Thunderbolt posture; the *only* asana permitted immediately after meals; enhances portal circulation.
  • • Dandasana: Staff pose; strengthens core abdominal and lower lumbar back extensors.
3. Prone Asanas (উপুড় হৈ শোৱা)
  • • Makarasana: Crocodile pose; deep psychosomatic diaphragm relaxation.
  • • Bhujangasana: Cobra pose; backward spinal extension, expands thoracic cage, stimulates thyroid.
  • • Shalabhasana: Locust pose; tones buttocks and lumbar muscles.
  • • Dhanurasana: Bow pose; intra-abdominal pressure massages pancreas and liver.
4. Supine Asanas (উত্তান হৈ শোৱা)
  • • Savasana: Corpse pose; conscious somatic stillness, triggers parasympathetic relaxation.
  • • Pawanmuktasana: Wind-relieving pose; relieves gas and abdominal bloating.
  • • Sarvangasana: Shoulder-stand; reverses venous pooling, bathes brain and thyroid in rich blood.
  • • Halasana & Chakrasana: Plow and Wheel; extreme spinal flexibility, tones adrenal glands.
3.3 Pranayama: The Vital Respiratory Technology Puraka • Kumbhaka • Rechaka

Three fundamental phases: Puraka (controlled inhalation), Kumbhaka (retention; internal or external), and Rechaka (controlled exhalation):

  • • Anulom-Viloma (Nadi Shodhana): Alternate nostril breathing without breath holding. Balances sympathetic (Pingala) and parasympathetic (Ida) hemispheres, reducing stress.
  • • Kapalbhati: Literally *"Skull Shining"*; rapid, forceful diaphragmatic exhalations with passive automatic inhalations. Clears frontal sinuses, dispels lethargy, and ignites digestive fire (Jatharagni).
  • • Bhastrika: *"Bellows breath"*; forceful, deep inhalations and exhalations generating profound metabolic heat and cellular oxygenation.
3.4 & 3.5 Mudras & Bandhas (Energy Seals & Locks) Neuro-Vascular Gates

Mudras redirect subtle psychic energy (Prana) through neuromuscular circuits:

Gyan / Chin Mudra: Thumb touches index finger; enhances memory, calms the mind.
Anjali Mudra: Palms folded at heart; fosters humility and inner devotion.
The Three Master Bandhas (Energy Locks):
  • • Jalandhara Bandha (Throat Lock): Chin rests on jugular notch; regulates carotid sinus pressure and thyroid.
  • • Uddiyana Bandha (Abdominal Lock): Abdomen pulled in and up towards spine; massages viscera.
  • • Mula Bandha (Root Lock): Perineal/pelvic contraction; channels energy upwards. (Maha Bandha combines all three!)
3.6 The Shatkriyas: Six Classical Yogic Purifications (ষট্কৰ্ম)
1. Neti: Nasal cleanse (Jala/Sutra)
2. Dhauti: Digestive tract wash
3. Nauli: Abdominal churning
4. Basti: Colonic cleansing
5. Kapalbhati: Sinus/frontal detox
6. Trataka: Ocular gaze focus
3.7 Japa vs. Ajapa (জপ আৰু অজপা)

Japa: Deliberate, conscious, repetitive recitation of a sacred mantra (Om, Gayatri, etc.) either aloud, whispered, or mentally.
Ajapa: Spontaneous, effortless, continuous breath awareness that occurs without voluntary mental effort—tuning into the natural "So-Ham" sound of human breathing ("So" on inhale, "Ham" on exhale).

Unit 4: Therapeutic Benefits of Yoga, Lifestyle Diseases & Systems Physiology

Supplemental/Regenerative Modes, Combating Psychosomatic Ailments & Anatomical Organ Impacts

Weightage: $5\text{ Marks}$

4.1 Four Functional Modalities of Modern Yogic Practice

1. Supplemental Yoga

Yogic routines practiced as an adjunct to professional sports or strenuous physical labour to optimize agility, spatial reflexes, and lung capacity.

2. Compensatory Yoga

Targeted postures engineered to reverse the structural deformities caused by modern sedentary desk jobs, repetitive keyboard strain, or standing all day.

3. Regenerative / Restorative

Passive, supported postures (using pillows, bolsters, wall-ropes) holding bodies in stillness to lower cortisol, stimulate lymph drainage, and convalesce post-illness.

4. Power Yoga

Vigorous, fast-paced modern fitness derivative based on Ashtanga Vinyasa; emphasizes dynamic calorie burning, core strength, and cardiovascular stamina.

4.2 Therapeutic Interventions for Contemporary Lifestyle Pandemics

Psychosomatic Medicine
1. Anxiety & Depression

Chronic stress triggers amygdala hyperactivity. Yoga activates the vagus nerve, boosting soothing neurotransmitters (GABA, Serotonin) and reducing serum cortisol.

Key Rx: Savasana, Bhramari, Yoga Nidra.
2. Obesity & Dyslipidemia

Stimulates sluggish thyroid metabolism, stimulates liver lipase activity, burns subcutaneous adipose tissue, and curtails emotional binge-eating.

Key Rx: Surya Namaskar, Kapalbhati, Paschimottanasana.
3. Essential Hypertension

Attenuates sympathetic hyperarousal, restores aortic baroreceptor sensitivity, and induces systemic peripheral vasodilation, lowering BP.

Key Rx: Savasana, Nadi Shodhana, Sheetali (Avoid headstands!).
4. Type-II Diabetes Mellitus

Torsional spinal twists and abdominal compression asanas directly massage the pancreas, stimulating the Islets of Langerhans to secrete insulin.

Key Rx: Ardha Matsyendrasana, Mandukasana, Dhanurasana.

4.3 Yoga and Human Self-Actualization

Humanistic psychologist Abraham Maslow posited Self-Actualization as the highest hierarchy of psychological human need—realizing one's supreme innate potential. Yoga offers the practical empirical methodology for attaining this state:

  • • Transcending the Ego (Ahamkara): Dissolves petty insecurities and defensiveness, cultivating unconditional universal empathy.
  • • Inner Coherence: Harmonizing thought, speech, and physical action into authentic ethical living.
  • • Peak Experiences (Samadhi): Unlocking profound states of inner peace, clarity, and creative intuition.
Yoga transforms an educator from an insecure worker into an inspiring, radiant presence!

4.4 Direct Physiological Impact Across Human Organ Systems

• Circulatory System: Enhances venous blood return to the heart; lowers resting pulse; prevents varicose veins.
• Musculoskeletal: Corrects spinal curvature (scoliosis/lordosis); increases synovial lubrication in joints; prevents osteoporosis.
• Digestive System: Peristaltic intra-abdominal pressure cures chronic constipation and enhances digestive enzyme secretion.
• Nervous System: Shifts autonomic tone towards parasympathetic dominance; promotes alpha and theta brainwaves.
• Respiratory System: Expands vital lung capacity; strengthens the diaphragm; improves alveolar oxygen diffusion.
• Excretory System: Promotes cellular metabolic waste evacuation via sweating, deep exhalation, and enhanced renal blood filtration.
